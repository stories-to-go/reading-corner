Latin for Medicine

Latin expressions that help learners understand medical language, diagnosis, therapy and the human body.

How to use this quiz

Choose the best answer. After every question, you will see a short explanation. The goal is not only to guess, but to understand the Latin expression.

Non scholae sed vitae discimus.

Meaning: We learn not for school, but for life.

Question 1

What does “mens sana in corpore sano” mean?

“Mens” means mind; “corpus” means body. The phrase connects intellectual and physical health.

Question 2

In medical language, what does “in vitro” literally mean?

“Vitrum” means glass. “In vitro” refers to processes outside a living organism, for example in a laboratory dish.

Question 3

What is the opposite of “in vitro”?

“In vivo” means “in the living”; in medical contexts it refers to processes in a living organism.

Question 4

What does “primum non nocere” express?

This phrase expresses a basic ethical principle in medicine: treatment should not create unnecessary harm.

Question 5

What does “diagnosis” originally suggest?

The Greek-Latin medical tradition often uses terms of knowing, seeing and distinguishing.

Question 6

What does “causa morbi” mean?

“Causa” means cause; “morbus” means illness or disease.

Question 7

What is the meaning of “status praesens”?

“Praesens” means present. In medical notes it points to the current state of the patient.

Question 8

What does “post mortem” mean?

“Post” means after; “mors/mortem” relates to death.

Question 9

What does “sine dolore” mean?

“Sine” means without; “dolor” means pain.

Question 10

What does “corpus” mean?

“Corpus” is one of the most important Latin words behind many modern terms.
