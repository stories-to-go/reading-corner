Latin for Philosophy

Latin expressions for truth, reason, human action and the search for wisdom.

How to use this quiz

Choose the best answer. After every question, you will see a short explanation. The goal is not only to guess, but to understand the Latin expression.

Non scholae sed vitae discimus.

Meaning: We learn not for school, but for life.

Question 1

What does “cogito, ergo sum” mean?

This famous Cartesian formula connects thinking with the certainty of existence.

Question 2

What does “tabula rasa” mean?

It is often used to describe the mind as not yet written on by experience.

Question 3

What does “sub specie aeternitatis” mean?

The phrase invites us to look at things from a timeless, broader perspective.

Question 4

What does “amor fati” mean?

The expression means accepting or even loving one’s fate.

Question 5

What does “veritas” mean?

“Veritas” is the Latin word for truth, central to many philosophical and academic traditions.

Question 6

What does “ratio” mean in philosophical contexts?

“Ratio” can mean reason, calculation, account or principle.

Question 7

What does “natura naturans” suggest?

The phrase points to nature as active, producing and forming.

Question 8

What does “natura naturata” suggest?

It contrasts with natura naturans and refers to nature as the produced order.

Question 9

What does “memento mori” mean?

It is a philosophical and spiritual reminder of mortality.

Question 10

What does “ars longa, vita brevis” mean?

The saying contrasts the lasting difficulty of art or skill with the shortness of human life.
