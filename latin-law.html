Latin for Law

Latin expressions useful for legal reasoning, evidence, responsibility and argumentation.

How to use this quiz

Choose the best answer. After every question, you will see a short explanation. The goal is not only to guess, but to understand the Latin expression.

Non scholae sed vitae discimus.

Meaning: We learn not for school, but for life.

Question 1

What does “in dubio pro reo” mean?

This expresses the idea that unresolved doubt should benefit the accused.

Question 2

What does “pacta sunt servanda” mean?

A central maxim in contract law and international law: agreements are binding.

Question 3

What does “bona fide” mean?

“Bona fides” means good faith; the expression is important in law and commerce.

Question 4

What does “de facto” mean?

“De facto” refers to reality in practice, even if legal recognition is different.

Question 5

What does “de iure” mean?

“Ius/iuris” means law. “De iure” means legally or according to law.

Question 6

What does “actus reus” refer to?

In common-law terminology, actus reus is the external element of a crime.

Question 7

What does “mens rea” refer to?

“Mens rea” is the mental element of a crime in common-law terminology.

Question 8

What does “habeas corpus” literally concerns?

Historically, it protects against unlawful detention by requiring a detained person to be brought before a court.

Question 9

What does “prima facie” mean?

A prima facie case appears sufficient at first view, before deeper examination.

Question 10

What does “res iudicata” mean?

It refers to a matter that has already been decided by a competent court.
